## The Speed Trap
Last Tuesday, I watched a cursor blink through a block of text I had rewritten four times. Each revision stripped away an adjective, inverted a clause, or deleted a sentence that tried too hard to be clever. It took forty minutes for three hundred words.
Later that same morning, an automated summary tool generated a ten-page synthesis of a complex regulatory shift in twelve seconds. It was coherent, polite, and completely hollow. It possessed syntax without stakes.
We live in an era obsessed with throughput. Speed is marketed as liberation, yet it often feels like an accelerated conveyor belt moving us past the exact things we wanted to examine.
> "Efficiency is intelligent, but speed without friction is just velocity toward nowhere in particular."
## The Cost of the Shimmer
Consider how we read now. Our eyes dart across glowing panes of glass, hunting for semantic density. We scroll past nuance, register outrage or validation in fractions of a second, and stash tabs into graveyard bookmarks we will never open again.
This isn’t stupidity; it’s adaptation. The environment rewards scanning.
- **Breadth over depth:** Knowing that a debate exists replaces the burden of understanding its architecture.
- **Reaction over reflection:** Engagement metrics reward the first sharp angle, never the second, wiser thought.
- **Accumulation over integration:** Collecting highlights Kindle-style feels like reading, the way buying running shoes feels like logging miles.
When everything is frictionless, nothing sticks. Depth requires resistance—the mental friction of a paragraph that doesn't immediately yield its point, or an argument that forces you to reverse your premise halfway through.
## The Counter-Practice
Reclaiming attention doesn't require smashing your phone or adopting a monastic lifestyle that collapses by Thursday. It starts with artificial friction.
Write the first draft longhand where your hand cramps and forces a slower cadence of thought. Read essays that take twenty minutes and offer no actionable productivity hack at the end. Sit on a bench for ten minutes without a podcast murmuring commentary into your skull.
Notice what happens in that minor vacuum. Anxiety flares first, disguised as boredom. Then, quietly, the mind stops panicking about what comes next and re-engages with the texture of the present room.
## Staying Human in the Slipstream
Technology won't slow down, and pretending we can vote ourselves back to 1994 is a nostalgic trap. The goal isn't refusal; it's sovereignty.
Decide which parts of your mental life you outsource to the conveyor belt and which parts you guard with barbed wire. Keep the slow hobbies. Write paragraphs that take too long. Let some thoughts remain half-formed, messy, and yours.
The future belongs not to those who can process a thousand summaries before breakfast, but to those who still know what it feels like to stay with a difficult question until it opens from the inside.
